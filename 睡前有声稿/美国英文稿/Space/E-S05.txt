Why Do Rockets Need Fire?

A toy rocket waits. "Where's the whoosh?"

Rockets push hot gas out the bottom very fast.

Push gas one way, and the rocket goes the other way.

That push-and-go idea is how rockets climb.

The bright flame is burning fuel making fast gas.

Fire here means energy turning into a strong push.

In space there is no road—only this push matters.

Wings need air; rockets can work without wings.

Real launches are loud, carefully planned, and grown-up jobs.

Toy whooshes are pretend—never play with real fire.

Gas out the back, rocket up the front—pair of forces.

Whoosh science: push down, rise up!
