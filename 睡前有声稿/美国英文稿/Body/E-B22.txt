Why Do We Shiver When Cold?

Brr! Shoulders shake. "Why shiver?" asks Guangguang.

Shivering is tiny muscle shakes that try to make heat.

Cold sensors tell your brain the body needs warming.

The brain sends a quick shake signal to many muscles.

Shaking uses energy and can raise temperature a little.

Goosebumps may join the cold response too.

A sweater, blanket, or warm drink helps more than shaking alone.

Come indoors from wind when you can.

Shivering after swimming is common until you dry and warm up.

If shivering comes with feeling very sick, tell a grown-up.

Your body has a built-in heater mode for chilly moments.

Sense cold, shake for heat, then get cozy: shiver science.
