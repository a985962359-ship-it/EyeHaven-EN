Why Do We Need Saliva?

"Spit is gross—why make it?" Guangguang holds a napkin.

Saliva is mostly water with helpful helpers mixed in.

It moistens food so chewing and swallowing feel easier.

Enzymes in spit begin breaking some starches early.

Saliva washes teeth and helps keep the mouth less dry.

It also carries taste bits to your tongue sensors.

A dry mouth makes talking and eating harder.

Drinking water supports healthy spit production.

Brushing still matters—spit is a helper, not a toothbrush.

Spitting at people is unkind; use a sink if needed.

Your mouth makes a quiet river of useful liquid.

Moisten, start digest, wash, taste: saliva jobs.
