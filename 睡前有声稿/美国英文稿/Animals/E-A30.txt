Why Do Snakes Stick Out Their Tongues?

A tongue flicks. Max jumps. “Gross—why?”

Snakes smell with their tongues. They pick up tiny scent bits from the air and ground, then read them with a special sense organ. Flicking helps them track prey and stay aware.

It looks rude to us. For snakes, it’s careful sniffing.

So a snake tongue is a smell antenna, not a tease.
