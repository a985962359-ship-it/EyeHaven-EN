Why Do Deserts Get Cold at Night?

Hot sand by day, chilly night! "Why the flip?"

Deserts often have dry air with little moisture.

Dry air does not hold heat as well after sunset.

With few clouds, heat escapes to the open sky.

Day sun bakes the ground; night lets warmth leave.

Temperature can drop a lot between noon and midnight.

Layered clothes help travelers handle the swing.

Animals may rest in shade by day and move at dusk.

Water and planning matter in desert trips with grown-ups.

Stars shine bright when desert nights turn clear and crisp.

Hot and cold can share the same sandy place.

Dry air, open sky, heat escape: desert night science.
