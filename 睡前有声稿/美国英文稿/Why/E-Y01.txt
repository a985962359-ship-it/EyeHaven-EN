Why Is Grass Green?

Guangguang sits on the lawn. “Why is grass green and not purple?”

Grass leaves have a tiny helper called chlorophyll. It catches sunlight—especially the red and blue parts—and uses that energy to make food for the plant. The green light mostly bounces back to our eyes, so grass looks green.

In fall, some plants stop making chlorophyll, and other colors show. Grass stays busy making food for a long time, so it stays green longer.

So green isn’t paint. It’s the color of a plant hard at work in the sun.
