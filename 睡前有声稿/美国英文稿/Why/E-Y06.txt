Why Do Bananas Turn Brown?

A yellow banana gets freckles, then brown. “Why?” asks Guangguang.

Bananas keep changing after they’re picked. A natural signal tells them to ripen. Soft enzymes break down the firm parts, and the peel’s color shifts from green to yellow to brown. Bruises brown faster where cells got squished.

Cooler air slows ripening; a warm bowl speeds it up. Brown spots can still mean sweet fruit inside—check with a grown-up if it smells okay.

So browning is ripening chemistry, not a banana “catching a cold.”
