Why Do Magnets Stick to Some Things?

Fridge magnet sticks. "Why not wood?"

Magnets pull on iron, nickel, and some steels.

Those metals have tiny magnetic regions inside.

A magnet can line them up and hold on.

Wood, plastic, and glass usually do not stick.

They lack those ready magnetic partners.

Two magnet poles can attract or push apart.

Opposite poles pull together; same poles push.

Magnet power fades with distance.

Compasses use Earth's big magnetic field.

Stick science is selective—not magic glue.

Try fridge, keys, and a wooden spoon—compare!
