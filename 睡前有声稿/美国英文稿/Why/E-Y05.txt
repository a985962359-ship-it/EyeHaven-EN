Why Do Magnets Stick to Some Things?

Guangguang holds a fridge magnet. “Why does it stick here, but not to wood?”

Magnets pull on materials with iron, nickel, or cobalt inside—like many fridge doors and some screws. Wood, plastic, and glass usually don’t have that kind of metal, so the magnet shrugs and slides off.

A magnet has two ends, often called north and south. Opposite ends pull together; same ends push apart.

So magnets aren’t sticky glue. They’re a quiet force that loves certain metals.
